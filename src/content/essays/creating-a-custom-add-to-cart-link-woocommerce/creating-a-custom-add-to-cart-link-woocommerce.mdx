<QuickSummary>

- Add `?add-to-cart=1234&quantity=1` to your store URL, replacing `1234` with the item’s ID.
- Use the product ID for a simple product and the variation ID for a specific variation.
- Grouped products need the group ID plus a separate `quantity[ID]` for each child product.
- Use your configured cart or checkout URL as the destination, and check the store’s redirect settings.

</QuickSummary>

To add one simple product to a WooCommerce cart from a link, start with this:

```text
https://example.com/?add-to-cart=1234&quantity=1
```

Replace `https://example.com/` with your store’s base URL, `1234` with your product ID, and `1` with the quantity to add. The numbers in every example below are placeholders, not IDs from your store.

These patterns follow [WooCommerce’s add-to-cart URL documentation](https://woocommerce.com/document/quick-guide-to-woocommerce-add-to-cart-urls/). The destination also depends on your store’s settings, as explained below.

## Simple Products

Find the product ID under **Products > All Products** in your WordPress dashboard. Hover over the product title to see its ID.

<Figure src={productId} alt={"The products list in Products > All Products, highlighting the product ID of a simple product."} />

Use that ID in `add-to-cart`, as in the opening example. To add three of the same product, change `quantity=1` to `quantity=3`.

## Variable Products

For a specific variation, use its **variation ID** instead of the parent product ID. Find it under **Product data > Variations** when editing the product. If you are not sure whether you need a variation at all, [the difference between attributes and variations](/woocommerce-attributes-vs-variations/) explains when they are useful.

<Figure src={variationId} alt={"The Variations tab of a variable product highlighting the variation ID."} />

If the variation ID is `5678`, the link is:

```text
https://example.com/?add-to-cart=5678&quantity=1
```

This selects that variation, rather than asking the customer to choose one on the product page.

## Grouped Products

Use the grouped product’s ID in `add-to-cart`, then specify each child product’s quantity separately:

```text
https://example.com/?add-to-cart=1234&quantity[5678]=5&quantity[7890]=2
```

Here, `1234` is the group ID. Replace `5678` and `7890` with the child product IDs. The example requests five of the first child product and two of the second.

## Cart, Checkout, Or Another Page

Put the query string after the URL where you want the customer to land. For a simple product, these examples use cart and checkout paths:

```text
https://example.com/cart/?add-to-cart=1234&quantity=1
https://example.com/checkout/?add-to-cart=1234&quantity=1
```

Replace `/cart/` and `/checkout/` with the actual paths of the pages configured for your store. For a variation, replace `1234` with its variation ID. The same approach works with another page on your store, using that page’s URL before the `?`.

WooCommerce’s [product settings](https://woocommerce.com/document/configuring-woocommerce-settings/products/#general) include **Redirect to cart page after successful addition**, under **WooCommerce > Settings > Products > General**. That setting can take the customer to the cart instead of leaving them on the requested page. Check it when choosing a destination, and verify the final behavior on your own store before sharing the link.

## If The Link Does Not Work

- **Wrong item or no item:** Recheck the ID under Products. A simple product needs its product ID; a specific variation needs its own variation ID.
- **Unavailable product:** Check its published status and stock settings. WooCommerce’s [product editor documentation](https://woocommerce.com/document/managing-products/product-editor-settings/) explains these controls, including stock status and backorders. An out-of-stock item that cannot be backordered is not available to purchase through a link.
- **Unexpected quantity:** Check stock availability and **Sold individually** in the product’s inventory settings. Sold individually limits purchases to one of that item per order; changing the URL quantity does not remove that restriction.
- **Wrong destination:** Confirm the configured cart or checkout page URL and the redirect-to-cart setting described above.

The URL patterns and settings here were checked against WooCommerce’s documentation, not tested on a running store. Store-specific behavior still needs a check in your own setup.
